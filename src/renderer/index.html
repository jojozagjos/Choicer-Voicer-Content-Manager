Choicer Voicer Content Manager

Version 1.0.2

This app does two jobs: it turns dubs you have already recorded into video files, and it builds the packs you record them with.

Turning a dub into a video

  1. Record in the game first. Play a voice pack and record your lines. Nothing appears here until you have done that at least once.
  2. Open Export and pick a pack. The first time, it spends a moment preparing the video for preview. That happens once per pack.
  3. Choose a recording session if you dubbed the same pack more than once.
  4. Press play, adjust anything that sounds off, then Export video.

Building a pack

  1. Click Content, then Create new.
  2. Open up the dub and fill it with content. Dub packs get the full timeline editor. Go to The Editor tab to learn more.

Already have a pack someone sent you? Unzip it and drop the folder straight onto the Content list. It works out what kind it is and installs it.

Dub packs open in a timeline editor. The official way to build one is to cut audio in a separate program and type timestamps in by hand. Here the clip and its timestamp come out of the video together.

Working the timeline

  • Click a clip to select it. Clicking empty space moves the playhead there and clears the selection. Dragging along the ruler at the top scrubs.
  • Hold still, then drag across empty space to cut a new clip out of the video. The pause is short, and it is what keeps an ordinary click from making a clip.
  • Drag the grip, the ribbed bar along the top of a clip, to move it.
  • Drag a clip's edge to change where it starts or ends. The audio is recut.
  • Right or middle drag to pan. Left dragging never moves the view, so you cannot knock a clip out of time by accident.
  • Scroll to zoom, shift and scroll to pan, double click to fit.
  • Space plays and pauses. ← → step two seconds, shift + ← → step a single frame, for lining a line up exactly.
  • Lines that overlap are stacked in their own lanes rather than hidden behind each other, so two people talking at once are both reachable.

The buttons

  • + Caption here cuts a clip at the playhead and puts the cursor straight in its caption box. Its length runs to the next line.
  • Captions draws the script over the video as it plays. There is a setting for whether this starts on.
  • Trim video cuts the video down to a range. See below.
  • Backing track builds the music and atmosphere track from the video's own audio by quietening it under each line.
  • Fit zooms the timeline back out to the whole video. F does the same.
  • The volume slider beside the clock is for listening here only. It changes nothing in the pack, and nothing about an export.

Trimming the video

A panel opens along the bottom of the picture, so you can watch the frame you are cutting on. Drag the two handles, or type into From and To, or scrub the video and press Use playhead for either end.

  • Every clip shifts with the picture, so a line stays against the frame it belongs to. A line that falls outside the part you kept is moved to the nearest edge rather than deleted, and you are told how many.
  • The cut lands on the nearest whole frame at or just before where you asked, never after it, so it cannot take a moment you meant to keep.
  • Most videos trim in a couple of seconds, because the picture is copied rather than re-encoded. A video that cannot be cut that way is re-encoded instead and takes longer, with a percentage while it works.
  • Both Undo and Redo cover it. The video from before the trim is kept aside, not overwritten.

The backing track lane

Once a pack has a backing track, a second lane appears under the timeline showing its waveform against the same stretch of time. It appears as soon as the track is built, without having to leave the pack and come back.

  • The waveform lines up with the clips above it, so you can see the dip under each line and check the ducking landed where you meant.
  • Listening to switches between the two. Video plays the original audio as it came; Backing track mutes the video and plays the track by itself. Only one is ever audible.
  • They are not played together on purpose. With both running, the original dialogue underneath made a track sound fine when it was not, which is the one thing you are listening for.

Neither choice changes anything in the pack. How loud the backing track sits in the game is set there, and on export by the Music slider.

About the backing track

Properly separating a voice from music needs a trained model far too large to ship inside an app like this. Instead, this uses something the pack already knows: exactly when every line speaks. It takes the video's audio and ducks those ranges.

  • Muffle dampens the audio so it still plays in the background, as if almost muted, keeping the atmosphere of the clip. This is typically the better option.
  • Silence removes it completely, which is cleaner but can sound like the audio dropped out.

Either way, music playing underneath a line is affected along with the voice. That is the limit of doing this without a model.

Pictures

Every line in the list has three buttons beside its picture:

  • Grab frame takes the video frame showing right now. Scrub to the moment you want, then press it.
  • Upload picks a picture file from your computer, converted to PNG on the way in.
  • Reuse lists the pictures already in this pack and points the line at one of them. The line shares that file rather than getting a copy, so editing the picture later changes every line using it.

Click any picture, anywhere in the app, to see it full size.

Lines are never given each other's pictures automatically, even when the character name matches, since two characters can share a name and look nothing alike. Reuse is always something you choose.

To cover a lot of lines at once, put a picture called _pack_filler_image in the pack folder. The game uses it for every clip with no picture of its own, and lines using it are marked with a dashed edge here. It covers the folder it sits in, so a pack sorted into subfolders wants one in each.

Undo and redo

The Undo and Redo buttons sit at the top of the editor, next to the pack name, and are greyed out when there is nothing to do. Ctrl + Z and Ctrl + Y do the same.

  • Everything is covered: moving, retiming, captions, characters, pictures, deleting a clip, and trimming the video.
  • Deleted clips are moved aside rather than removed, and a trimmed video is kept, so both can be put back.
  • Undoing something that takes real work, like a trim, shows the same overlay the original did rather than appearing to freeze. If a step cannot be carried out it stays on the button, so it can be tried again.

While it is working

Building a backing track or trimming a video takes a moment, and an overlay covers the editor while it does.

  • The video will not play behind that overlay, or while the trim panel is open. The file it would be playing is the one being rewritten, and the trim panel is there to pick a still frame.
  • Leaving the editor, or switching tabs, stops the work rather than letting it finish out of sight. Nothing in the pack is changed by a job that was called off.

Reference, not required reading. You do not need any of this for general use: the app knows what each pack should contain and tells you when something is missing. It is here for anyone who wants to know what the files mean.

The game reads seven kinds of pack, each from its own folder.

Voice and dub packs
packs_voice. A video plus the lines to dub over it. With a video it is a dub pack; without one it is a plain voice pack. This is the type with the full editor.
Players
packs_player. A picture, a colour pair, and up to nine reaction sounds. You can record those straight into the app.
Hosts
packs_host. The presenter's voice lines and portrait.
Judges
packs_judges. The judging panel's pictures and lines.
Studios
packs_studio. The 3D set, as a .glb model.
Menus
packs_menu. Backgrounds, music and styling for the game's menus.
Chatter
packs_chatter. Crowd lines that play between rounds.

Every pack you open is checked against what the game actually accepts, and anything wrong is listed with what to do about it. A red mark means the game will not use it as intended; amber is worth a look; grey is a note.

Every line has its own controls in the list on the right: which take to use, how loud it is, and how far to nudge its timing.

  • MP4 works everywhere. Use WebM only if you specifically need it.
  • Vertical 9:16 reformats for Shorts, TikTok and Reels, filling the sides with a blurred copy of the video instead of black bars.
  • Burn captions draws the script onto the video permanently. Leave it off and tick the .srt box instead if you would rather add captions in an editor.
  • Normalise dub loudness evens out lines recorded too quietly or too loudly.
  • Selected line only exports a single line as a short clip. Click a line first.

Exports run one at a time. Queue as many as you like and they will work through in order.

  • No packs listed. The game folder is wrong, or you have not recorded a dub yet. Check the folder in Settings.
  • A pack shows "no dubs yet". That pack is installed but you have not recorded for it. Do that in the game first.
  • A pack will not open. If it is still converting, it stays locked until that finishes. The tile shows a progress bar while it works.
  • Export button is greyed out. The pack is still loading. Wait for it to finish.
  • Preview is black. Switch packs and back again to rebuild the preview. If it keeps happening, tell me on Discord.
  • Export failed. Usually a full disk or a folder you do not have permission to write to. Try exporting somewhere like your Videos folder.
  • Windows warned about the app on first run. That is because the app is not signed with a paid certificate. Click More info then Run anyway.
    Two things about this app look suspicious to Windows on their own: it reads files it was not obviously given permission to read, and it writes files it was not obviously given permission to write. That is the whole job, since the packs and recordings it works on belong to the game rather than to it. Nothing leaves your computer.
